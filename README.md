# AI Research Engineer Academy

A practical, evidence-driven learning path from Senior Software Engineer to **AI Engineer / Applied AI Engineer**, and then toward **AI Research Engineer**.

## Career strategy: AI Engineering first, Research Engineering next

The near-term priority is to build production-ready AI engineering skills and a portfolio that can support a transition into applied AI roles. Research engineering remains the long-term direction: understanding papers, reproducing methods, optimizing models, and eventually developing original ideas.

These stages are directional learning horizons, not guaranteed hiring timelines:
- **Stage 1 — Applied AI Engineering (roughly months 1–12):** Python, LLM applications, RAG, evaluation, deployment, reliability, and production systems.
- **Stage 2 — Research Engineering (roughly months 12–24+):** ML foundations, PyTorch, Transformers, fine-tuning, paper reproduction, GPU systems, and independent experiments.

Start exploring suitable Applied AI opportunities as soon as the portfolio demonstrates relevant skills; do not wait for Week 104.

## Goals

- Build practical Python skills while leveraging existing senior software engineering experience.
- Design, evaluate, and operate LLM-powered applications.
- Build production-quality RAG and AI backend systems.
- Develop mathematical, ML, and PyTorch foundations in parallel with applied work.
- Understand Transformers, fine-tuning, and model evaluation.
- Read arXiv and Google Scholar papers critically.
- Reproduce selected research results and document deviations.
- Develop GPU, performance, and ML systems skills.
- Complete an independent research project and a credible public portfolio.

## Weekly commitment

Plan for **5–7 hours per week**, using 6 hours as the default:
- 1.25 h — mathematics and theory
- 2.75 h — implementation
- 0.5 h — experiments and evaluation
- 0.5 h — notes and portfolio
- 1 h — MAANG interview preparation

Math continues alongside applied engineering. It is important, but it should not become a reason to postpone all practical AI work.

## Learning loop

1. Understand the problem and assumptions.
2. Learn the minimum theory needed to make progress.
3. Implement a working baseline.
4. Test correctness and edge cases.
5. Run controlled experiments and evaluate quality.
6. Document trade-offs, limitations, and next steps.

## Portfolio targets

1. **Production RAG system:** ingestion, chunking, embeddings, retrieval, reranking where useful, evaluation, tests, observability, latency/cost tracking, Docker, and a .NET integration.
2. **Classical ML pipeline:** reproducible data preparation, regression/classification, baselines, metrics, and error analysis.
3. **Transformer and fine-tuning project:** PyTorch implementation, training loop, adaptation, and evaluation.
4. **Research reproduction:** independent implementation, controlled experiments, comparison with the paper, and a reproduction report.
5. **ML systems optimization:** serving, profiling, GPU/memory measurements, and performance analysis.

## Target roles

**Near-term roles to assess:** Senior AI Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, AI Product Engineer, AI Platform Engineer, ML Platform Engineer, AI Infrastructure Engineer, and suitable AI Forward Deployed Engineer roles.

**Long-term target:** AI Research Engineer.

Role titles vary across companies. Evaluate actual responsibilities and requirements rather than relying on titles alone.

Progress is recorded only when supported by completed work. The starting state remains **0/104 weeks, 0 study hours, Week 001 Not Started** until evidence is added.

**Build useful AI systems first. Understand the methods deeply. Reproduce research. Then contribute something new.**
